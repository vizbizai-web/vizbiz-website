# VizBiz Brand Voice & Copy Standards

The single rule everything below derives from:

**We only say what the pipeline can prove — and we say it plainly.**

This is not a style preference. It is the business model. VizBiz sells
to people (lawyers, owners, dealers) who are pitched hype every single
day. The copy that closes them is the copy that sounds like nobody
else's inbox: calm, specific, backed by a screenshot. Trust is the
conversion mechanic; the voice is how trust sounds.

---

## 1. Positioning line

Primary:
> **VizBiz shows you exactly what ChatGPT, Gemini, and Perplexity say
> when your customers ask for a business like yours — and fixes what's
> keeping you out of the answer.**

Short: "Be the business AI recommends."
Never: "revolutionary AI-powered visibility platform" or any sentence
that could belong to a competitor.

## 2. Voice principles

**P1 — Evidence before adjective.** Every claim carries its receipt.
- ✗ "Dramatically improve your AI visibility"
- ✓ "You appeared in 2 of 54 answers. Here's each one we tested."

**P2 — Name the enemy.** Abstract loss doesn't move anyone; a rival's
name does. When the data shows a competitor winning, say the name.
- ✗ "Your competitors may be outranking you in AI search"
- ✓ "Martin Law was named in 6 of the 18 Hamilton questions. You
  appeared in 1."

**P3 — Plain words, owner's vocabulary.** Write for a plumber at 7am
and a managing partner between depositions. Jargon only when the
reader's industry owns it (SABS to a PI lawyer: yes; "GEO/AEO" to
anyone: no, say "AI search").
- ✗ "Optimize your entity's structured data for LLM ingestion"
- ✓ "Make your services page something AI systems can actually read."

**P4 — Calm confidence, zero exclamation.** We never sound excited to
sell. We sound like someone who has the data and doesn't need volume.
No exclamation marks in any client-facing surface. No urgency theater
("Act now!", countdown framing). The finding creates the urgency.

**P5 — Admit the edges.** Limits stated plainly are a trust weapon,
not a weakness. "We test three platforms" beats a vague "all AI."
"Your score hasn't moved yet" in a monthly report is what makes the
month it does move believable.
- ✓ "AI answers vary run to run. Small monthly wiggles are noise;
  the trend line is the signal."

**P6 — We do the work; we say so simply.** The differentiator is
artifacts, not advice. Copy should always land on the finished thing.
- ✗ "Actionable recommendations to improve your schema"
- ✓ "The schema file is attached. Your web person pastes it in.
  Twenty minutes."

**P7 — Respect over pressure.** Every outreach includes a clean way
to say no. Every CTA is an invitation, not a trap. Three touches,
then silence. This is both CASL compliance and brand.

## 3. Vocabulary

**Use:** appear / show up, named, tested, verified, the answer, your
customers ask, fix, files, evidence, baseline, trend, we checked,
re-scan, "here's what we found."

**Banned (marketing-speak):** unlock, supercharge, game-changing,
revolutionary, cutting-edge, seamless, empower, leverage (as a verb),
skyrocket, 10x, dominate, crush, "in today's digital landscape."

**Banned (honesty class — watchdog-enforced):** any platform not
actually tested; any prompt/business count not from real runs; dollar
loss figures without client-specific defensible math; "guaranteed
rankings"; study claims outside the registry.

**Internal words that never reach a client:** operator, pipeline,
preflight, fallback, auto-discovered, Mission Control, provider names
(Firecrawl, Sonar as a product name), lead (say "your report"),
approval queue.

## 4. The five trust mechanics that close (use everywhere)

1. **Proof-first sequencing.** The reader sees their own data before
   any pitch. Outreach leads with the finding; the report page leads
   with the answer; the call leads with the screen-share. We never ask
   for money before showing evidence.
2. **The named rival.** The single highest-converting element we have.
   Subject lines, report highlights, monthly alerts — when a real
   competitor appears in the data, their name does the selling.
3. **The re-scan guarantee.** "Same questions, same platforms, 30/60
   days later — you see the movement, or you see us say it hasn't
   moved." This sentence, nearly verbatim, belongs on the pricing
   page, in the close, and in the Fix Kit roadmap.
4. **The unflattering number, kept.** When the data is bad for us
   (score didn't move, client appeared fine before buying), we show
   it. One honest "no change yet" report buys ten months of retention
   credibility.
5. **The visible mechanism.** Always one sentence of *why this works*
   in plain terms — "AI systems recommend businesses they can read,
   verify, and see proof for" — so the buyer feels informed, not
   dazzled. Informed buyers subscribe; dazzled buyers churn.

## 5. Surface patterns

### Outreach email (cold, audit-led)
- Subject = the finding, ≤ 12 words, rival named when true.
- Body ≤ 120 words: who we are (1 line), what we tested + what we
  found (2–3 lines, their numbers), why it's fixable (1 line), one
  soft CTA ("want the report?"), identification + opt-out line.
- Never attach the sale to email 1. The report is the ask.

### Transactional emails (delivery, welcome, fix kit)
- Subject: "{Business}: " prefix + plain statement of what's inside.
- Structure: what this is (1 line) → the one number that matters →
  what to do next (1 action) → what happens after (1 line). Sign-off
  from Alex by name. No postscripts, no upsell in delivery emails —
  the report page carries the CTA.

### Monthly one-pager
- Voice of a good employee reporting to the owner: "Here's the month."
- Order: number + trend arrow → what changed (their prompts, named
  competitors) → what we did → what's next. Every number from the
  diff engine, none hand-written.
- When flat: say flat. "No new appearances this month. Here's what
  we're changing." (P5 in action — this line retains subscribers.)

### Report pages
- Headline states their situation, not our product: "ChatGPT names
  your competitor. Here's why — and the fix." Locked/paid sections
  labeled with what's inside, not "upgrade to unlock" language:
  "The 9 fixes we found — included in the Fix plan."

### Fix Kit artifact cards
- Three beats, fixed order: **What this is** (one plain sentence) →
  **Why it matters** (one sentence tying to their audit finding) →
  **What to do** (numbered steps or "forward this to your web
  person"). Time estimate on every card. No card exceeds one screen.

### Pricing page
- Tier names describe the outcome, not the mechanism: "The Fix" ($88),
  "Fix & Monitor" ($188/mo), "Done For You" ($497/mo, founding $397).
- Each tier: one sentence of who it's for, the deliverables as nouns
  (files, reports, calls — things), the guarantee under the monthly
  tiers. No feature-grid arms race, no "MOST POPULAR" badge theater
  unless the data ever proves one.

### CTAs
- Verbs that describe what the click does: "See the full results" /
  "Get the fix files" / "Start monitoring" — never "Get started",
  "Learn more", "Unlock now."

## 6. Formatting rules

- Sentences short. One idea each. Paragraphs ≤ 3 sentences in email.
- Numbers honest and concrete: "2 of 54", "up to 20 prompts per
  platform", never rounded up, never vague-precise ("dozens").
- Percentages get their base: "15% (3 of 20 prompts)".
- Dates on everything evidential: "tested January 14" — dated evidence
  reads as real because it is.
- No emoji in client-facing copy. (Telegram operator alerts: fine.)
- Second person throughout: "you appeared", "your services page."

## 7. Rewrites (calibration examples)

| Before | After |
|---|---|
| "Unlock your AI visibility potential with our cutting-edge audit!" | "We tested 18 questions your customers ask AI. You came up once. Here's the report." |
| "Your business could be losing thousands in revenue every month!" | "When someone asks ChatGPT for a plumber in Etobicoke, it names three of your competitors. Not you." |
| "Our proprietary 84-prompt methodology across 5 AI platforms" | "Up to 20 real buyer questions, tested on ChatGPT, Gemini, and Perplexity." |
| "Actionable insights and recommendations" | "Seven files, ready to install. Your web person needs about an hour." |
| "Congratulations! Your report is ready!!" | "Mop Wringers: your AI visibility report is ready." |
| "Don't fall behind your competitors in the AI revolution" | "These answer positions are being claimed now. The firms that structure for AI first tend to keep them." |

## 8. Governance

- qa:client-copy is the mechanical enforcer: platform claims, study
  claims (registry-gated), banned internal vocabulary. Extend it with
  the marketing-speak banned list above (warn-level, not block-level,
  for the soft words).
- Any new number in client copy must trace to pipeline data, Stripe,
  or the studies registry. If it can't, it doesn't ship.
- New surface = write it in this voice, then read it as the skeptical
  owner at 7am. If it sounds like an ad, cut until it sounds like a
  report.
- This file lives at docs/launch/vizbiz-brand-voice.md and is
  referenced in every copy task given to any agent.
